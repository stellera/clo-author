# Claim–Evidence Rules

## Named design with a stated identifying assumption (shock, cutpoint, instrument, randomization) plus a pre-trend, balance or placebo check

**Allowed claims:** causal_claim at low hedging where the design is named, counterfactual and policy-sized statements that stay inside the sampled population

**Preferred verbs:** estimate, identify, exploit, instrument, difference out, absorb

**Avoid:** mechanism attribution without a channel-level operation, generalization beyond the population the design binds

The check, not the design label, is what licenses the low hedge.

## Regression output with no stated design

**Allowed claims:** associational statements at the sample's own descriptive strength, conditional-correlation statements that name the controls that carry the comparison

**Preferred verbs:** correlate with, is associated with, predicts, loads on

**Avoid:** causal_effect wording, using controls as though they were an identification argument

The corpus never promotes coefficient output to causal force in the absence of a warrant paragraph.

## Heterogeneity, targeting or gradient evidence

**Allowed claims:** suggestive_causal mechanism statements explicitly labelled as such, scope statements about where the effect concentrates

**Preferred verbs:** is consistent with, suggests, points to, tracks

**Avoid:** demonstrates that the channel operates, treating a subgroup pattern as a test of the mechanism

The single most frequently violated rule in draft text; it is also the corpus's clearest boundary.

## Direct channel test (a specification, component isolation or mediator estimate run for the channel itself)

**Allowed claims:** mechanism_claim statements tied to that test

**Preferred verbs:** tests, decomposes, isolates, operates through

**Avoid:** importing the average effect's causal label into the channel sentence, claiming the channel explains the whole effect

Only 8 of 10 mechanism profiles reach this strength for any given channel.

## Model, derivation or calibration only

**Allowed claims:** theoretical statements about what the object implies, counterfactual magnitudes explicitly labelled as model-derived

**Preferred verbs:** imply, deliver, quantify under the assumed structure

**Avoid:** causal identification from fitted parameters, policy magnitudes presented as empirical effects

Theory slots stay near-unhedged about the model and heavily hedged about the world.

## Institutional or descriptive fact (dates, coverage, eligibility rule, construction)

**Allowed claims:** unhedged statements of the fact itself, scope statements about what the rule applied to

**Preferred verbs:** stipulates, took effect, covers, requires

**Avoid:** behavioural interpretation of the rule before the question is framed, hedging the documented fact

Hedging documented facts is itself an error in this corpus (see AP-CLAIM-02).

## Null or non-significant result

**Allowed claims:** no support detected at the stated precision, bounds on the detectable effect in the named subsample

**Preferred verbs:** find no evidence, is imprecise, bounds, rules out effects larger than

**Avoid:** equal to zero, disproof of the rival, elimination of a channel

Absence of an observed contrast is not a positive causal verdict.

## Citation, precedent or documented prior finding

**Allowed claims:** positioning statements, justification for a specification or estimator choice, comparison of magnitudes in comparable units

**Preferred verbs:** builds on, departs from, is closest to, reconciles

**Avoid:** validity of the paper's own identifying assumption, novelty claimed from absence alone

Volume of citations licenses nothing about assumptions.

## Self-built measure, index or imputed variable

**Allowed claims:** construct-validity statements backed by a displayed check, measurement-error statements that bound direction as well as size

**Preferred verbs:** construct, impute, validate against, bound

**Avoid:** using the constructed object as evidence before its provenance paragraph, treating a construction choice as measurement-neutral

Data-section grammar requires source, measure, check in that order.

## Convergent estimates across independent routes

**Allowed claims:** robustness statements about the estimand, confidence that the finding is not an artefact of one specification

**Preferred verbs:** converges, survives, is stable across

**Avoid:** treating convergence of specification variants as independent confirmation, adding the variants' precises as if they were replications

Independence has to be argued, not inferred from a table.
