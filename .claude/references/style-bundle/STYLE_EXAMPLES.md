# Synthetic Style Examples

All examples are generated abstractions, not quotations from source papers.

## baseline readout with translation

The estimate implies a [direction] effect of [size] on [outcome] in the sampled population. Expressed against the pre-period mean, that is [translated magnitude], which is [comparator statement].

**Why:** Demonstrates the quantity-plus-unit cadence (HARD-RESULT-01, HARD-RESULT-03) without any specific result.

## heterogeneity kept suggestive

The effect is larger among [subgroup], which is consistent with [channel] operating; the gradient is not a direct test of that channel.

**Why:** Shows the licensing boundary in claim_evidence_rules entry 3: gradient evidence earns 'consistent with', not 'demonstrates'.

## null reported at its own strength

We detect no effect on [outcome]; the estimate is precise enough to rule out changes larger than [bound], which leaves [rival] untested rather than excluded.

**Why:** Implements the null rule: bounds and untested rivals, no disproof claim.

## assumption with an implication attached

The design requires that [assignment mechanism] be unrelated to [outcome path] absent the treatment. If that held, we would expect [observable implication]; the [balance or pre-trend display] checks it directly.

**Why:** Implements HARD-STRATEGY-02: the warrant is paid for with an implication and a check, not with a hedge.

## robustness check with a scope verdict

Restricting the sample to [subset] leaves the estimate at [value]. This bounds the role of [feature] rather than supporting a claim about [untested population].

**Why:** Implements HARD-ROBUST-01: the verdict states what the check bounds.

## introduction preview after the gap

Whether [X] causes [Y] is unresolved because [measurement or identification obstacle]. Using [device], we estimate a [quantity] effect on [outcome] in [setting], which is [translated magnitude].

**Why:** Shows the framing, gap, then quantified preview order required by HARD-INTRO-02/03/04.

## conclusion verdict separated from boundary

Paragraph 1: the paper's answer about [object] is [verdict at the body's strength]. Paragraph 2: that result speaks to [scope] only under [assumption], and lifting it would require [data or institution].

**Why:** Implements HARD-CONCL-01: verdict and scope in different paragraphs, limitation paired with what would lift it.

## data restriction disclosed where created

The series is available from [year] for [coverage]; records before [year] are absent because [institutional reason], so estimates for [period] rest on [subsample] rather than the full population.

**Why:** Implements HARD-DATA-01: provenance and limit at the point of use, stated unhedged.
