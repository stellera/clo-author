# Synthetic Elite Economics Voice Examples

All examples are generated abstractions. They are not quotations from source papers.

## Question + design
> We study whether firms with more diversified supplier networks absorb trade shocks more effectively. We exploit pre-shock differences in sourcing structure and compare firms within narrowly defined industries.

## Quantified result
> A one-standard-deviation increase in supplier diversification raises post-shock import growth by 4.5 percentage points, about 20 percent of the baseline mean.

## Heterogeneity with bounded mechanism language
> The effect is concentrated among firms that had alternative origins in place before the shock. This pattern is consistent with pre-existing substitution capacity facilitating adjustment.

## Identification threat
> A concern is that diversified firms were already on different growth paths. Event-study estimates show little differential movement before the shock.

## Null
> We find no detectable employment response over the first two years, and the confidence interval rules out declines larger than 2 percent.

## Robustness
> Restricting the sample to firms continuously observed throughout the period leaves the estimate nearly unchanged. This makes differential attrition an unlikely explanation for the baseline result.

## Literature position
> Existing work shows that firms reallocate suppliers after trade disruptions, but it does not separate exposure to the shock from the capacity to substitute. Our design isolates that distinction.

## Conclusion scope
> The evidence identifies short-run adjustment among incumbent exporters. Whether the same mechanism governs firm entry remains open because entry requires data outside the current sampling frame.
