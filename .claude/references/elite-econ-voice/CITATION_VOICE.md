# Citation Voice

## Principle
Citations support propositions. They should not become the grammatical subject of a catalogue unless chronology, disagreement, or lineage is itself analytically important.

## Preferred patterns

### Strand-level positioning
> A large literature studies how trade shocks affect local labor markets [citations].

### Named nearest neighbor
> Our design is closest to Author (Year), who uses X to identify Y.

### Contrast
> Unlike studies that infer adjustment from aggregate flows [citations], we observe supplier changes at the firm-product level.

### Magnitude comparison
> The estimate is smaller than the effect reported by Author (Year) in a setting with substantially higher baseline exposure.

## Avoid
> Author A studies X. Author B studies Y. Author C studies Z.

unless the sequence itself is the point.

## Citation placement
Prefer:
- citation immediately after the proposition it supports;
- named textual citations when the identity of the prior paper matters;
- parenthetical clusters when multiple papers support the same strand-level claim.

Avoid:
- citations used to defend the paper's own identifying assumption;
- long citation clusters attached to vague claims.
