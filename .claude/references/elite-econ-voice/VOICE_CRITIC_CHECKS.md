# Elite Voice Critic Checks

- **EVC-01** Does a substantive paragraph open on an economic/research object rather than metadiscursive throat-clearing?
- **EVC-02** Are consequential research actions expressed with a clear actor and a specific verb where agency matters?
- **EVC-03** Do reporting verbs match their rhetorical jobs, or are synonyms being used merely for variety?
- **EVC-04** Is each important magnitude integrated with the object it quantifies and, where useful, a meaningful comparator?
- **EVC-05** Are central contrasts written in visibly matched syntax?
- **EVC-06** Is hedging concentrated on attribution, reach, and interpretation rather than spread across facts and estimates?
- **EVC-07** Does clause order mirror the research logic without forcing the reader to backtrack?
- **EVC-08** Are method bridges compact enough to state object/variation, research action, and estimand without ceremonial prose?
- **EVC-09** Are result paragraphs opened by the substantive result or estimand rather than by a table/figure announcement?
- **EVC-10** Do paragraph closers state an inference, bound, resolved threat, or handoff rather than generic significance?
- **EVC-11** Are transition words semantically necessary, or are they stacked mechanically?
- **EVC-12** Is "we find" used functionally rather than repetitively?
- **EVC-13** Are citations attached to propositions rather than arranged as an author catalogue?
- **EVC-14** Are evaluative adverbs doing work that should instead be done by evidence or magnitude?
- **EVC-15** Is passive voice obscuring a research action that would be clearer in active form?
- **EVC-16** Does the prose imitate recognizable source wording, metaphors, or signature phrases? Flag and rewrite structurally.
- **EVC-17** Is there one dominant argumentative job per paragraph?
- **EVC-18** Does any voice preference conflict with Scientific Style claim-evidence discipline? If yes, Scientific Style wins and no voice deduction applies.
