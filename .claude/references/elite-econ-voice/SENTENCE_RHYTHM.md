# Sentence Rhythm

No fixed sentence-length target is imposed. The ensemble pattern is functional variation.

## Core rhythm
Prefer:
1. one sentence that establishes the object or claim;
2. one sentence that supplies design, evidence, or magnitude;
3. one sentence that interprets, bounds, or transitions when needed.

Not every paragraph needs all three.

## Main-clause priority
Put the main research action or result early enough that the reader does not need to hold multiple subordinate clauses before discovering the sentence's point.

Preferred:
> Using pre-reform sourcing shares, we estimate the effect of the tariff shock on firm imports.

Less preferred:
> Using pre-reform sourcing shares, which vary considerably across firms and products and are measured before the policy change, the effect of the tariff shock on firm imports is estimated.

## Dense + landing sentence
A longer design sentence can be followed by a shorter result or implication sentence.

Synthetic pattern:
> Because exposure varies across products within firms and across firms within industries, the specification absorbs both firm and industry-year confounders while retaining the relevant cross-product contrast. The estimates remain stable.

## Parallel comparison
When contrast is central, use matched clause structure.

Synthetic pattern:
> Employment falls among firms with concentrated supplier bases but changes little among firms with diversified sourcing.

## Avoid monotony
Do not force:
- every sentence to 20–25 words;
- every sentence to start with "We";
- identical three-sentence paragraphs;
- repeated "This result..." closers.

## Parentheticals
Use parentheticals for compact technical information, not for the main inferential step.

Good uses:
- units;
- sample restriction;
- brief definition;
- table/figure reference.

Avoid putting the substantive interpretation in parentheses.
