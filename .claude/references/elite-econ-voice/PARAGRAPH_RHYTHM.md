# Paragraph Rhythm

## Governing principle
One paragraph should have one dominant argumentative job. Variation comes from how that job is executed, not from stacking unrelated moves.

## Result paragraph
Preferred cadence:
1. substantive result;
2. quantity / uncertainty;
3. magnitude translation or comparator;
4. interpretation at licensed strength;
5. optional handoff.

## Identification paragraph
Preferred cadence:
1. source of variation;
2. identifying assumption;
3. threat;
4. implication/check.

## Mechanism paragraph
Preferred cadence:
1. channel/rival;
2. discriminating prediction;
3. channel-specific test or gradient;
4. bounded interpretation.

## Robustness paragraph
Preferred cadence:
1. what changes;
2. result under perturbation;
3. what the check rules out or bounds.

## Literature paragraph
Preferred cadence:
1. strand-level proposition;
2. what that strand can establish;
3. unresolved margin;
4. how the present design changes the evidence.

## Conclusion paragraph
Prefer argument-level closure:
- verdict;
- scope;
- implication;
rather than replaying paper sections.

## Openings
Good openings name:
- the result;
- the comparison;
- the threat;
- the data object;
- the prior-literature capability.

Avoid:
- "In this paragraph..."
- "It is important to note..."
- "Another interesting result is..."

## Closings
Good closings:
- state the bounded implication;
- resolve a threat;
- identify the next analytical object;
- state the scope condition.

Avoid:
- generic importance statements;
- vague "taken together" summaries when the inference can be stated directly.
