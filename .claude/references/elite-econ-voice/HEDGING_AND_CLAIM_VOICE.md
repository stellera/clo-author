# Hedging and Claim Voice

This file controls linguistic realization only. Evidence strength is governed by the Scientific Style Bundle.

## Direct / low-hedge slots
Use relatively direct wording for:
- documented institutional facts;
- sample definitions;
- data construction;
- treatment assignment;
- estimated quantities;
- algebraic/model implications conditional on the model.

Preferred forms:
- "The law took effect in..."
- "We construct..."
- "The estimate is..."
- "The model implies..."

Avoid decorative softeners:
- "appears to have taken effect"
- "may perhaps indicate" when reporting an estimate already shown.

## Causal result slots
When the Scientific Style Bundle licenses causal language:
- prefer "we estimate", "we find", "the reform increased/decreased";
- keep the causal force in the design and warrant, not in intensifiers.

Avoid:
- "proves"
- "conclusively demonstrates"
- "definitively establishes"

## Suggestive mechanism slots
Preferred:
- "is consistent with"
- "suggests"
- "points to"
- "tracks"
- "is more pronounced where..."

Avoid:
- "demonstrates the mechanism"
- "shows that the channel operates" unless a direct channel-specific design licenses it.

## Null-result slots
Preferred:
- "we find no evidence of..."
- "the estimate is imprecise"
- "the interval rules out effects larger than..."
- "we cannot reject..."

Avoid:
- "there is no effect"
- "the mechanism is absent"
- "the rival is disproved"

## External-validity slots
Preferred:
- "the evidence speaks most directly to..."
- "whether the result extends to... remains open"
- "the mechanism may travel to settings where..."

Avoid:
- unqualified "these findings apply broadly"
- vague "the results are generalizable"

## Model-based counterfactuals
Preferred:
- "the model implies..."
- "under the estimated structure..."
- "the counterfactual suggests..."

Avoid:
- presenting simulated magnitudes as design-identified empirical effects.

## Hedge placement
A paragraph should not have uniform uncertainty. Put hedging on:
- attribution;
- extrapolation;
- rival interpretation;
- mechanism;
- welfare/policy interpretation when not directly identified.

Do not hedge:
- dates;
- sample sizes;
- coverage rules;
- estimator definitions;
- reported coefficients;
- source provenance.
